Embeddings: Turning Meaning Into Numbers

An embedding is a list of numbers (a vector) that represents the meaning of a piece of text. Texts with similar meanings get vectors that are close together, even if the wording is completely different.

The friend-explanation

Imagine every chunk of your documents is placed somewhere in a giant room. Similar chunks stand near each other: phrases about "blood pressure" all cluster in one corner, phrases about "third-party risk" in another. An embedding is simply the coordinates of where your chunk stands in that room. When you ask a question, we convert the question to coordinates too, then walk to the nearest cluster. Those nearby chunks are your search results.

Similarity: cosine distance

We compare vectors with cosine similarity: are they pointing in the same direction? The result is a number between −1 and 1 (in practice roughly 0 to 1 for text embeddings). Higher = more similar. Our code ranks chunks by this score and returns the top k.

You do not need to compute this by hand; one line of vector math finds the closest chunks even among thousands.

Dimensions

Vectors are typically 384-way up to 3072-plus numbers long. More dimensions generally means richer meaning but slower indexing. Local-friendly models commonly produce 384–768 dimensions, which is plenty for personal document search and fast on a laptop.

What our examples do

Real model weights are hundreds of megabytes and downloading them in a training-free handbook is rude packaging. So examples/01-index.py ships with a deterministic mock embedder: a hashing-based vector that stands in for a real one. It is genuinely useful for structure (same words → same vector) but it does not truly understand synonyms.

The moment you install a real embedder (see Chapter 06), you flip one flag and everything else in the pipeline stays identical. That is the point: the embedder is a swappable part, not the architecture.

Pitfalls

  • Mixing models. Never compare vectors from two different models — they live in different "rooms" entirely. One index, one model, forever.
  • Re-index on model change. If you upgrade the embedder, rebuild the whole index; old vectors are meaningless to the new model.
  • Long inputs. Models truncate silently past their limit. Chunk first (Chapter 01) so nothing important is silently dropped.
  • Headings carry meaning. Prefix a chunk with its document title ("Manual: breast-care procedures — step 3") and retrieval gets noticeably better for one line of code.
